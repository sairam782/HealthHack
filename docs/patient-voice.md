# Gentle, task-aware patient voice

Kalinga is a request assistant. It helps patients describe needs and contact the right care team. It does not act as a nurse, diagnose symptoms, recommend medication, promise an arrival time, or tell a patient that they are safe.

## Conversation behavior

- Greeting: “Hello, I’m Kalinga, your request assistant. I’m listening. Tell me what you need.”
- Recap: recognized intents are summarized in plain language. Multiple needs remain separate, such as pain and water. Unclear requests receive an honest “I’m not sure I understood everything”; arbitrary instructions are not read back as a staff update.
- Pain: the Pain button, submitted typed pain request, and finalized explicit pain report in manual or hands-free voice send to Nursing immediately. Clinical rules bypass model calls. Only after the server commits the ticket and staff notifications does Kalinga ask for an optional 1–10 score. A selected or spoken score updates the **same** Nursing ticket and alerts the team again. It preserves real staff acknowledgement and linked service tasks; support teams do not receive pain details. A score already stated in the first request is retained without asking again. Skip, cancel during the follow-up, and timeouts skip only the rating: the delivered pain request stays active. Unclear ratings get one hands-free retry. Dose, room number, decimal, out-of-range and ambiguous answers are rejected. Explicit pain denials avoid this automatic pain flow and retain conservative clinical routing.
- Corrections: in hands-free mode, “also…” adds a need, and “change my request to…” replaces the draft. Non-pain draft revisions require an explicit send command; a revised explicit pain report sends immediately. Spanish, Hindi and French equivalents are supported.
- Emergency: known urgent pain sends immediately without a score question and includes bedside call-bell guidance. New urgent symptoms during a pain follow-up raise the existing request to P1 and trigger the server’s medical escalation; a low score never lowers urgency. Other voice drafts retain their explicit confirmation flow. The on-screen urgent button sends directly.
- Timing: audio playback pauses hands-free recognition to prevent the assistant from hearing itself. After a prompt ends, the listener resumes. The listener allows 60 seconds after prompt playback for a reply. Non-pain draft timeouts leave the unsent draft on screen. Pain follow-up timeouts state that the request remains with Nursing. Microphone permission, muted speech, errors and manual stop remain under patient control.
- Status: receipts use gentle wording, including “I’m sorry you’re hurting” for pain and “Thank you for telling me” for other requests. Team acknowledgements and completions remain grounded in real database actions. An assistant conversation never invents staff acknowledgement or an arrival time.

The score card appears after notification and scrolls into view. Patients can tap a rating, add optional location/change details, choose **Update Nursing**, or answer through **Voice input**. Hands-free ratings save automatically. **Update pain report** on an open non-emergency Nursing ticket reopens this card after skipping or reloading. Resolved requests require a new request. Visible text mirrors the spoken prompt; the voice preference controls readback. Hands-free is unavailable when spoken replies are disabled.

The patient-only pain endpoint verifies ticket ownership and uses a revision check plus the existing transactional lock. Identical retries do not duplicate alerts; stale conflicting reports fail instead of overwriting newer information. Post-delivery speech prompts require a server-owned ticket and use persisted pain values, so a client cannot invent delivery or staff acknowledgement.

## Language and speech

Manual voice now defaults to **Multilingual recording** when Groq is configured and the browser supports MediaRecorder. Select **Voice & reply language**, tap **Speak in [language]**, speak, and tap **Stop voice input**. The recording also stops after 30 seconds. Hindi, Spanish, **Español (México)** and French have localized recording controls. Mexican Spanish retains the `es-MX` locale throughout the app; Whisper receives the ISO language `es`.

The patient-only `/api/transcribe/recording` endpoint sends the clip to Groq `whisper-large-v3` using transcription, preserving the original language. It accepts at most 1 MB of audio in bounded JSON, validates format, limits attempts, and rejects empty or low-quality segment results. These checks cannot guarantee recognition accuracy. Kalinga does not persist audio; the provider processes it. Changing language, signing out, hiding the page or joining a call cancels capture/pending transcription. Pain transcripts use the existing immediate Nursing flow; routine transcripts remain drafts until confirmed.

**Browser live speech** is available as an alternative. Hands-free wake detection still uses the browser or separately configured AWS streaming; the recorded path does not add hands-free Whisper. Existing ElevenLabs spoken replies are retained. Physical phone/iPad microphones, accents and short score answers still need device/native-speaker testing.

Hands-free wake detection now handles split greeting/name results, punctuation, and recognition alternatives. Patients using English browser recognition can choose a regional speech setting and inspect the latest microphone transcript under **Voice options**. See [real-speaker wake acceptance checks](wake-acceptance.md) for the limits, test protocol, and next provider comparison. This does not establish accent accuracy or enable AWS Transcribe.

The new conversational prompts are localized in English, Spanish, Hindi and French. Other languages retain their existing request/status readback; the app does not insert an English conversational question for those selections. Native-speaker and physical-device testing are still required.

Speech continues to use ElevenLabs multilingual v2 with the existing voice. Requests set speed to `0.94` and stability to `0.65`, aiming for a slightly slower and consistent delivery. This is a design choice, not a validated accessibility preference; patients should help evaluate pacing. See [ElevenLabs voice settings](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/voices/settings/get) for the speed parameter.

## How the “intelligence” works

`patient-assistant.js` formats bounded prompts from recognized intents and reported pain; `wake-voice.js` manages follow-ups, edits, confirmation and cancellation. The same formatter is used by the browser's visible dialogue and the authenticated patient-only synthesis endpoint. Patient request routing uses the existing clinical safety rules and the configured model ensemble for applicable nonclinical requests; see [expert routing](expert-routing.md). Conversational follow-ups remain bounded templates rather than an open-ended medical chatbot.

`tests/gentle-dialogue.test.cjs` covers multilingual rating parsing, retention of needs across turns, refusal to invent scores, skipping, urgent bypass, explicit revisions, cancellation, bounded retries, unsupported prompt types and instruction-like input. Browser QA uses simulated recognition transcripts with real synthesis and native Audio playback. It does not validate physical microphone recognition, accents, clinical quality, perceived gentleness, or every language on a real patient.

## Readable text and response cue

The patient request box uses 22px text, a 190px minimum height, and generous line spacing. Spoken prompts use 22px text, staff updates use 20px, and the hands-free status is a larger card in both themes. When capture, pain-rating, or confirmation listening is ready, it displays **Your turn — speak now** (localized in English, Spanish, Hindi and French).

A soft, brief two-note chime sounds once per response turn, after prompt playback finishes and recognition reports microphone startup. Background wake monitoring and routine recognition restarts do not chime. Calls, hidden pages, microphone denial, and blocked prompt playback suppress the cue; stopping cancels pending sound. Manual Voice input also chimes when recognition starts. Under **Voice options**, patients can switch **Sound when it is my turn to speak** off while retaining the visual indicator. No network provider is used for this sound. The audio context is unlocked by the patient’s hands-free/microphone gesture, following [Web Audio guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices). Device volume and browser audio permissions still affect audibility.

Six added regression tests cover turn timing, single-turn cues, pause/stop/hidden cleanup, recognition restarts, soft tone scheduling, unsupported audio and pending-resume cancellation. Isolated browser QA uses simulated recognition and silent generated prompt audio with native Web Audio tones; it verifies timing and sound-off behavior. Physical iPhone/iPad audibility still needs a device check.
